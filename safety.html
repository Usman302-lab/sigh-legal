Breathing safety

Last updated: 10 November 2026

Sigh is a wellness practice, not a treatment. If you feel dizzy, tingly, cramped or unwell, stop and return to your normal breathing. Do not use breath-holding in water or while driving.

Before you start

  • Sit or lie somewhere safe and comfortable.
  • Do not force the breath or chase a number.
  • Keep the exhale gentle; never strain.
  • Stop if you feel light-headed or panicky.

When to be cautious

Speak to a clinician before practising breath-holding or rapid breathing if you have a heart, lung, seizure, blood-pressure or panic-related condition, are pregnant, or are recovering from surgery. Sigh is not designed for people in acute crisis.

No medical claims

Sigh does not diagnose anxiety, insomnia or any condition, and does not replace therapy, medication or emergency care. If you are in crisis, contact your local emergency service or a crisis line.
